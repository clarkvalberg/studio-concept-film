# Concept Film Frameworks

This is the canonical structure for studio concept films and the three variants that map to different concept types. Read this in Phase 3 before writing the script.

## The canonical structure

Every concept film has five sections. The proportions shift by variant but the structure is invariant.

| Section | Default duration | Purpose | What it must do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold Open** | 0:00–0:08 | Pull attention | Establish a single concrete image, moment, or question. No setup, no exposition. |
| **Problem / Context** | 0:08–0:22 | Earn the right to speak | Show the friction in the current world. Specific, not generic. |
| **Insight** | 0:22–0:35 | The turn | The conceptual shift. "What if…" / "We saw that…" / "Here's what's actually true…" |
| **Product Walk** | 0:35–1:05 | Make it real | Show the product working. Name the parts. Ground the insight in something the viewer can almost touch. |
| **Vision Close** | 1:05–1:25 | Aspiration | Earned by everything prior. Brand mark + tagline + final image. |

**Total budget: ~85 seconds = ~210 words at standard VO pace (≈150 wpm).**

For a 60s film, compress proportionally — Cold Open shrinks to ~5s, Vision Close to ~10s. For a 120s film, expand the Product Walk; never expand Vision Close past 20s (it gets indulgent).

## The three variants

### Variant A — Customer-led

**Use when:** the concept lives in a human moment. Healthcare, education, housing, services, anything where the user experience *is* the product. Common examples: a clinician doing home visits, a housing operator running a portfolio, a teacher in a classroom. The viewer enters through empathy.

**Cold Open archetype:** a person, mid-moment. Not posed, not introduced. We see them before we know what we're looking at.

> "It's 7:14 PM. Maria is on her third call about the broken radiator on unit 4B."

**Problem section:** stay with the person. The friction is theirs. We feel it through them.

**Insight section:** widen out. Name the structural truth their moment reveals.

> "There are 2.3 million units like 4B in this country. The operators running them are working harder than they've ever worked."

**Product Walk:** return to the person, but now with the product in their hand. Show how it changes their next move.

**Vision Close:** end on what becomes possible. Often the person, lighter; sometimes a wide shot of the category they're part of.

**Tonal anchor:** warmth without sentimentality. Don't manipulate; observe.

### Variant B — Insight-led

**Use when:** the concept's power is conceptual or structural. B2B platforms, infrastructure, tools whose human moment is hard to dramatize without contrivance.

**Cold Open archetype:** a question, a statement of fact, or a striking visual abstraction.

> "Most software is still built around the assumption that someone is typing."

**Problem section:** elaborate the consequence of the status quo. Stay conceptual but specific.

**Insight section:** the turn happens here, and it lands harder because the viewer has been thinking with you.

> "But the language of work has changed. We're no longer typing — we're describing."

**Product Walk:** demonstrate the insight materialized. Real screens, real interaction. This is where the abstract becomes touchable.

**Vision Close:** project the insight forward. "If we're right, then…"

**Tonal anchor:** confident curiosity. The film is thinking out loud with the viewer, not lecturing.

### Variant C — Demo-led

**Use when:** the product is visually striking enough that the viewer's first reaction will be "what is that." Generative tools, novel interfaces, anything where the screen is the hook.

**Cold Open archetype:** the product in motion, cold. No voiceover for the first 2–4 seconds. Let the viewer be confused.

> [4 seconds of a strange, beautiful interface doing something the viewer doesn't yet understand]

**Problem section:** brief. The viewer is already engaged; long context is a tax. Often skipped entirely or compressed to one line.

> "Designers have always had to choose between speed and quality."

**Insight section:** name what they just saw.

> "What you're looking at doesn't make that choice."

**Product Walk:** longer than usual. The product is the protagonist. Show multiple capabilities, layered.

**Vision Close:** brief, confident. The product has already done the work.

**Tonal anchor:** assured. The film knows it's interesting; it doesn't have to convince anyone.

## How to pick the variant

Three signals:

1. **Where does the energy of the concept live?**
   - In a human moment → A (Customer-led)
   - In an idea → B (Insight-led)
   - In the product itself → C (Demo-led)

2. **What's the audience's starting state?**
   - Need to be emotionally pulled in → A
   - Need to be intellectually engaged → B
   - Need to be surprised → C

3. **What does the source material lead with?**
   - User stories or interviews → A
   - Thesis or argument → B
   - Product demo or prototype → C

If two signals point to one variant, that's the variant. If signals conflict, default to A — it's the most forgiving and produces the highest hit rate.

## Worked examples (mini)

### Customer-led example — Harmony (LIHTC operations)

```
COLD OPEN (0:00–0:07)
[On-screen: hands sorting through a thick paper file]
VO: It's the 14th of the month. Russ has 312 compliance documents
    sitting on his desk.

PROBLEM (0:07–0:22)
[On-screen: spreadsheets, paper forms, a tired office]
VO: Affordable housing in America runs on paperwork.
    Every unit, every tenant, every month. Audits that take quarters.
    Penalties that compound. Operators who got into this work for
    the mission, spending their days on compliance.

INSIGHT (0:22–0:35)
[On-screen: shift to clean interface]
VO: The compliance work isn't going away. But the part where humans
    do it line by line — that's a choice. Not a requirement.

PRODUCT WALK (0:35–1:05)
[On-screen: Harmony product, real screens]
VO: Harmony reads the documents. Catches the gaps. Flags the
    deadlines. Surfaces what needs attention, when. Russ doesn't
    chase paperwork. He runs his portfolio.

VISION CLOSE (1:05–1:20)
[On-screen: wide shot of a residential street, then product logo]
VO: 2.3 million affordable units. One operating system.
    Harmony.
```

### Insight-led example — generic B2B platform

```
COLD OPEN (0:00–0:06)
[On-screen: cursor blinking in an empty text field]
VO: We've spent forty years teaching humans to speak the language
    of computers.

PROBLEM (0:06–0:20)
[On-screen: cluttered enterprise UI screens]
VO: Buttons. Menus. Forms. A thousand small fluencies just to do
    the work you were hired to do.

INSIGHT (0:20–0:32)
[On-screen: clean interface, single input]
VO: But the computers can speak our language now. So why are we
    still speaking theirs?

PRODUCT WALK (0:32–1:05)
[Real product screens]
VO: [Product] lets you describe what you want. The work happens.
    The result is the artifact. The interface is the intent.

VISION CLOSE (1:05–1:20)
VO: Software for people who have things to do. [Product name.]
```

## Common structural failures (and how to avoid them)

**The film tells but doesn't show.** Symptom: voiceover names things, but the visuals are stock or generic. Fix: every line of VO must have a corresponding concrete visual idea. If you can't think of one, the line shouldn't exist.

**The insight is too small.** Symptom: the "turn" doesn't feel like a turn — it's just a feature description. Fix: the insight section must name something structurally true about the world, not just a feature of the product.

**The product walk is a feature list.** Symptom: "It does X. And Y. And Z." Fix: pick the two or three capabilities that ladder to the insight. Show them. Don't tour the menu.

**The vision close is grandiose.** Symptom: "Reimagining the future of work." Fix: vision close should be specific and confident, not abstract and aspirational. Earn the aspiration through the specifics that came before.

**Pacing is even.** Symptom: each section feels the same weight. Fix: concept films have rhythm. The cold open is short. The problem builds. The insight is a *moment* — often the shortest section by word count. The product walk is the longest. The vision close lands hard and gets out.
