# Philosophy

This is the long version of the README's "Why it exists" section. Read it if you want to know what the skill refuses to be.

---

## What a concept film is for

A concept film is the moment a concept becomes legible. Not legible to the team that built it — the team can hold the concept in their heads from a Notion doc and a Figma file. Legible to someone outside the team: an LP, a prospective hire, a customer in the first conversation, a partner who needs to make a decision in eight minutes.

Most ideas die because they never get legible to anyone outside the room they were born in. The concept film exists to close that gap. It's a thinking artifact, not a marketing artifact. The difference matters.

A marketing artifact is built to persuade. A concept film is built to clarify. Persuasion follows for free if the clarity lands. The inverse is not true.

<br />

## What this skill refuses to be

**It is not a "make me a video" tool.** You bring source material. The skill turns it into one specific genre: 60–90 seconds, voiceover-driven, prototype-grounded, insight-led. If you want a tutorial, an ad, a recorded demo, a social post, or a five-minute explainer, this is the wrong skill. The narrowness is the point.

**It is not a wizard with a thousand options.** The skill picks. You confirm or correct. There are three structural variants, not twelve. There are eight curated voices, not eighty. There is one canonical structure with named variants, not a builder. Choice paralysis is the enemy of good work; curation is the value-add.

**It is not a chatbot pretending to be a tool.** The skill behaves like software. It gates. It decides silently where it can. It refuses to advance past failed legibility checks. It withholds options. It tells you which phase you're in. It does not ask three questions when one would do.

**It is not a "magic" black box.** Every artifact it produces is editable. The script lives in a Markdown file you can edit. The design tokens live in a TypeScript file you can tune. The Remotion project is yours to refine. The skill is the starting line, not the finish.

## The form's defining qualities

The form this skill produces has five non-negotiable qualities. These are extracted from the films that worked, not invented.

### 1. The product is the protagonist's tool, not the protagonist's identity

The film does not say "people who use [product] are different kinds of people." It says "here is a person doing their work, and the product is in their hand." The product earns its place by being useful, not by being aspirational signaling.

This is the difference between a SaaS commercial that aged well and one that aged poorly. The ones that aged well showed real people doing real work. The ones that aged poorly showed lifestyle.

### 2. The voiceover speaks **to** the viewer, not **at** them

The voice is warm-confident. Adult. Slightly aspirational without ever being breathless. It treats the viewer as an intelligent peer who can fill in gaps on their own.

The voice is **not**:
- The "epic" register — sweeping music + grand claims. Reads as parody now.
- The "best friend" register — performatively casual. Reads as a podcast ad.
- The "demo" register — feature-listing, present-tense, breathless. Reads as a webinar.

### 3. Real screens, not abstract metaphors

The product is shown. Viewers should leave knowing what it looks like, what it does, and what the core ontology is. Abstract metaphors (gears, lightbulbs, geometric forms representing data) are signals of a team that hasn't done the work yet.

Schematic stand-ins are acceptable when real screens don't exist — but the film flags it as a constraint, not a feature.

### 4. The music builds; the film lands

The film has rhythm. The cold open is short. The problem builds. The insight is a *moment* — often the shortest section. The product walk is the longest. The vision close lands hard and gets out.

Pacing is not even. Even pacing reads as flat. The film must breathe, then move, then breathe again.

### 5. Aspiration is earned through specificity

The vision close lands on something specific the product enables, not something abstract it represents. "Software for people who have things to do" can only describe one product. "Reimagining the future of work" describes none.

The film earns its closing line through every specific that came before. If you cut the closing line and pasted it into a competitor's film, it should not fit.

## The principle of recognition over persuasion

A concept film succeeds when the viewer experiences recognition. Not agreement. Not conviction. Not a buying signal. Recognition — the moment they think *"yes, that's the thing I've been trying to name."*

Recognition is harder to engineer than persuasion. Persuasion can be brute-forced with rhetorical pressure. Recognition cannot. Recognition requires that the film accurately *names* something the viewer was already half-thinking, and presents it back with clarity the viewer didn't have themselves.

Everything in this skill — the legibility gate, the banned words, the insight section, the warm-confident voice — exists to engineer recognition.

Persuasion is downstream. If the recognition lands, persuasion follows. If the recognition doesn't land, no amount of persuasion compensates.

<br />

## Why the banned words list

Words become banned not because they lack meaning but because they have been worn through. "Revolutionary" once meant something. "Game-changing" once described actual category shifts. "Leverage" once described actual asymmetric advantage. These words have been emptied by relentless overuse in pitch decks, product pages, and earnings calls.

Using them today is not a stylistic choice. It is a category signal. It signals that the film was written by someone who has not yet noticed how much language has changed. The viewer notices. The film loses the room before the idea has had a chance to land.

The banned words list is the cheapest possible quality intervention. Removing nineteen words from a script gets you 40% of the way to a film that doesn't feel generic. The remaining 60% is harder.

## On craft

The frameworks in this skill are extracted, not invented. The structures, the timing budgets, the variant taxonomy, the voice shortlist — all of it traces to films that worked, plus the principles that explain why they worked.

This is what makes the skill defensible. The form is not novel. The codification is. Anyone can make a concept film. Few make one that lands. The difference is craft, and craft is the thing this skill is trying to compress into something portable.

The skill is not a substitute for craft. It is craft made repeatable.

<br />

## A final note on tools and judgment

A skill that picks for you is not a skill that thinks for you. The skill picks the variant, but you confirm. The skill writes the script, but you edit. The skill chooses the four voices to audition, but you select. The skill renders the hook, but you decide if it lands.

The judgment remains yours. The skill compresses the time between judgment moments, removes the friction of starting from blank, and codifies the rules that catch you when you're about to ship something generic.

That's the point.
